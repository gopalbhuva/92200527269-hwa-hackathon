Product

The Orbit is a mechanical enrichment puzzle for dogs. Three rotating rings sit around a slow-feed core; you hide kibble or treats in the channels and pockets, set the difficulty, and your dog works the puzzle out with their nose and paws.

There are no batteries, motors or app. It's designed to be left out, cleaned in the dishwasher and used every day.

Twist the rings to change how open the treat channels are. Level one leaves treats visible and easy to reach. Level two narrows the channels and slides the middle ring. Level three locks the pockets and rotates the sequence so your dog has to work for every piece.

Kibble, small training treats, freeze-dried pieces or a mix. The core holds up to two cups of kibble. For a longer hunt, pack the core with a full meal and use a few higher-value treats in the outer pockets.

Yes. Start puppies and older dogs at level one, with a smaller amount of food and a supervised first session. The low profile means most dogs can work it without jumping or stretching.

Safety & care

The Orbit is designed for solo enrichment and has no cords, batteries or small removable parts. As with any new toy, supervise the first few sessions and choose a difficulty that suits your dog's chewing style. No toy is indestructible — if your dog is a determined chewer, start supervised and check the puzzle regularly.

Food-grade silicone, BPA-free recyclable polymer and a weighted recycled base. All materials are chosen for daily contact with food and dogs, and the puzzle is free from small parts and sharp edges.

Twist the rings apart and place everything on the top rack of your dishwasher. For a quick clean, warm water and a soft brush are enough. The materials won't hold onto smells or flavours.

The Orbit is chew-resistant, not chew-proof. Most dogs engage with the puzzle rather than the object, especially when the difficulty is matched to their confidence. If your dog likes to chew the puzzle itself, start at an easier level, supervise, and use the two-year warranty if anything fails.
